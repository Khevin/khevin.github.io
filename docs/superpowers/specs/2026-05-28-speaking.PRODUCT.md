## 1. Register & layout (the binding decisions)

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **`register:`** | `editorial` |
| **`layout:` (studio screen)** | `spread-with-bottom-rail` — asymmetric watercolor hero LEFT, practice stage RIGHT, Up Next phrase filmstrip ACROSS THE BOTTOM |
| **`layout:` (Speaking section nav)** | `triple-rail` — class rail (existing) → book rail (Speaking → Shadowing) → category rail (Food, etc.) → phrase rail (6–14 per category) |
| **`layout:` (Pitch & Tones basics page)** | `editorial single-scroll` with rail-and-body inside — inherits the existing Writing-section pattern (sentence-structure, particles), uses progressive disclosure with worked examples |

Editorial register is inherited from the existing Nihongo brand (paper canvas, sumi-e accents, vertical typography, three-role type, monochrome chrome with color confined to artwork). The Shadowing studio runs *product mechanics inside editorial chrome*: a mic, waveforms, and score cards exist — but they live INSIDE brush-edged surfaces, sit ON the paper canvas, never dominate the watercolor portrait, and never violate the project's no-emoji / no-shadow-DOM-ish-dashboard rules.

The **spread-with-bottom-rail** pattern earns its choice because the screen needs three simultaneous jobs: (1) an emotional anchor that makes the learner WANT to speak (the watercolor portrait — a presence to speak to, not just a UI), (2) a practice stage where the eye lives during each repetition (phrase + mic + waveforms + scores), and (3) a continuation signal that turns one phrase into a session (Up Next). Theatre-with-caption-rail compresses (1) into a chip — wrong tradeoff for an editorial brand. Rail-and-body forces (2) and (1) to compete in the same column — also wrong.

The **triple-rail** navigation extends the existing nihongo sidebar pattern (vocab class → book → page). Speaking gets a category rail (Food first; later Greetings, Travel, Work) and inside each category a list of 6–14 phrases. The user explicitly asked for this shape; it also fits the editorial table-of-contents instinct.

The **Pitch & Tones basics page** is the teaching companion. Without it, the OJAD-style contour over each phrase is decoration; with it, learners can decode WHY は starts low and じ jumps high. It lives under `Writing → Pitch` as the last item in the basics section, alongside the existing sentence-structure and particles essays.

## 2. Users

Three personas inherited from the project, plus one additive specific to speaking.

### 2.1. The self-directed adult learner (inherited)

> *Evening at the kitchen table, headphones on, repeating はじめまして for the tenth time — watching the pitch line rise on は and dip on じめ, listening to their own waveform against the model — chasing the feeling of speaking Japanese the way Japanese sounds.*

The same person from the flavors brief. Roughly N5 kanji depth. Comes back daily. Wants ACQUISITION through repetition — speaking is the second sense (after seeing the kana) that locks the word into memory. Has 5–15 minute sessions on the speaking surface specifically (shorter than the contemplative flavors sessions; speaking is tiring).

**Drives:** the keep-the-portrait-warm-and-present rule; the auto-play-once-then-on-demand audio behavior; the score-as-encouragement framing (numbers visible but never blocking).

### 2.2. The first-timer (inherited)

> *Two weeks in. Hiragana mostly stuck. Just learned はじめまして in a phrasebook. Wants to know if they're saying it right. Cannot yet identify a pitch fall by ear.*

Assumes nothing about kanji or pitch literacy. The system must work cold — audio leads, kana shows with furigana, pitch contour drawn explicitly above the mora row (don't hide it; the learner needs to see what they should be listening for). English gloss is one tap away but never on the front.

**Drives:** the pitch contour as a teaching diagram, not just a chrome flourish; the "Tap to Record" prompt explicit on the mic; the kana-with-furigana default; the link from any phrase straight to the Pitch & Tones basics page when the learner taps the pitch contour itself.

### 2.3. The mobile learner (inherited)

> *Couch, evening, one-handed, headphones. Wants the mic right under the thumb. Doesn't want a UI element collapsing when they tap by mistake.*

No hover affordances. Touch targets ≥ 44px. The Up Next rail scrolls horizontally with momentum. The portrait CAN collapse on mobile (it becomes a small shoulder thumbnail at top); the practice stage stays full-width. The waveform pair stacks vertically on small viewports instead of side-by-side. Audio playback works without the mic ever being granted permission (you can listen-only).

**Drives:** the mic-bottom-thumb-zone rule; the no-confirm-modals contract; the mobile portrait-collapse breakpoint; the recordings auto-delete from memory between phrases (so RAM doesn't bloat over a long session).

### 2.4. The pitch-aware re-learner (NEW, additive)

> *Three years in, can read Japanese, but their pitch accent is flat — every word sounds gaikokujin (foreigner-shaped). Comes to Shadowing specifically to fix this. Doesn't care about the watercolor; cares deeply about the contour accuracy and whether their pitch score moves when they fix the fall.*

Power user. Wants Pitch surfaced as a distinct dimension (not buried in Naturalness). Wants the contour to be CORRECT — atamadaka vs heiban actually marked differently, not visually identical. Wants the score to be diagnostic, not just a vibe. May leave the platform if the pitch dimension is decorative.

**Drives:** Pitch as the 4th explicit score dimension (already locked at Gate 0); the OJAD-correct contour notation (already locked at Gate 0); the pitch-extraction in scoring being real (autocorrelation pitch detection on the user clip), not a fake number; the "this phrase is heiban / atamadaka / nakadaka / odaka" classification chip beside the contour.

### Accessibility — backstop constraint

The mic-permission gate must be clearly communicated and recoverable. If permission is denied, the entire RIGHT column drops to a listen-only / pitch-study mode — the page never feels broken because the user said no to the mic. Pitch contour color cues are paired with the contour LINE itself (shape > color, so a colorblind learner sees the same up/down). Waveforms render as both shape AND a numeric duration ("02:10") so a screen reader has something useful. `prefers-reduced-motion` swaps the recording pulse for a static red dot. The Recording state is announced via `aria-live`.

## 3. Brand personality (inherited from the project)

The three project pillars apply unchanged — **Sensory · Patient · Disciplined** (from the flavors brief). I won't re-litigate them. What matters for Speaking is how each pillar lands here:

- **Sensory** — the watercolor portrait isn't a decoration; it's a *presence to speak TO*. Every phrase is rendered at display size (clamp 64–96px) so the kana fills the eye before the audio plays. The waveform is sensual: real shape, real movement, not abstracted bars.
- **Patient** — no timer. No streak counter. No "level up." The Up Next rail is a continuation, never a push. If the learner records 30 times in a row before moving on, the page applauds (visually) without nudging them forward.
- **Disciplined** — pitch contour is drawn correctly per OJAD convention OR not drawn at all. Scores are heuristic-real, never random. The mic permission UX never tricks. No emoji as feedback. The "92" in the score circle is a number, not a celebration.

## 4. Restrictions (what this surface is NOT)

Bans, drawn from the project's existing list plus speaking-specific:

- **No emojis as score feedback.** No 🎉 / 🎊 / ⭐ on a "you did great." The score is the number.
- **No streak counters, no XP, no daily-goal nag.** Patience pillar.
- **No leaderboard, no social.** This is a solo studio.
- **No saving the user's audio across sessions.** Memory only. The clip exists for the playback comparison; it dies on next-phrase or page-leave. The user is told this at the mic-permission moment.
- **No server / no cloud STT in v0.8.** Heuristic scoring on the client. Listed as v1.0+ work.
- **No fake scores.** Every number displayed must derive from a real signal (real duration delta, real volume envelope, real pitch autocorrelation). Heuristic is fine; random is not.
- **No "perfect" verdict.** The page never declares the learner correct. It scores, it visualizes, it lets the learner judge.
- **No countdown timers in the recording UI.** The mic listens until you tap to stop or until 8 seconds of silence (whichever first). No "3-2-1 GO" anti-pattern.
- **Pitch contour is OJAD-style or absent.** No invented notation. Either we draw it the way every Japanese textbook draws it, or we don't draw it.
- **No interaction inside the watercolor portrait.** It's a presence, not a control surface. No "tap the portrait to skip" or hover effects on her face. Restraint preserves the editorial weight.

## 5. Pedagogical pillars (speaking-specific, layered on the brand)

Four rules that drive every interaction:

### 5.1. Listen first, twice.

Auto-play the model once on phrase entry. Make the second play one tap away. The mic is grayed until the model has been heard at least once (visually grayed, not blocked — the user CAN bypass, but the default flow asks them to listen).

### 5.2. Pitch is teachable, not magical.

The contour is drawn every time, on every phrase, even on simple greetings. The learner sees the line on phrase 1 of session 1 and continues to see it on phrase 200. Repetition trains the eye. The Pitch & Tones basics page is one tap away from any contour.

### 5.3. The score is feedback, not a verdict.

Four dimensions: Rhythm, Clarity, Pitch, Naturalness. Each is heuristic-derived from one specific signal. Each can be improved by ONE specific thing the learner can DO. No composite "overall" score that hides which dimension to work on. The 92/100 in the top-right corner of the mockup is the rolling session score — present but secondary.

### 5.4. Repetition is the unit.

The session is "tap a phrase, listen, record, listen back, record again, listen back, move on." Not "tap, listen, record, get a verdict, advance." The user controls when to move on. Up Next is always available; never required.

## 6. Sequenced build plan (referenced at Gate 8)

See companion `DESIGN.md` for the token system and component anatomies, and the build skill for the actual execution order. The sequence locked here:

1. **Sidebar plumbing first.** New main-nav category `Speaking`, books array with `Shadowing` placeholder, sidebar 2 (categories), sidebar 3 (phrases). No content yet — verify the navigation works empty.
2. **Pitch & Tones basics page** (Writing section). Standalone teaching surface, simpler to ship and unblocks the contour notation everywhere else. Includes the four pattern names (heiban/atamadaka/nakadaka/odaka) + worked examples + pause/notation legend.
3. **Shadowing data model.** Phrase shape (kana, kanji, romaji, en, pitch contour data, category id). Seed 6–10 phrases from existing food sentences (already in the project).
4. **Shadowing studio shell.** Three zones (portrait, stage, filmstrip) wired with placeholder phrase content. No mic yet.
5. **TTS playback.** Web Speech API integration, model playback with the existing TTS settings (voice, rate, volume). Auto-play-once + replay button.
6. **Mic capture + recording.** MediaRecorder + AudioContext. Real waveform draw on the user's row. Permission UX.
7. **Heuristic scoring engine.** Four dimensions, each from a real signal. Number renders in the score chip with a 1–5 dot strip below (also from the mockup).
8. **Pitch contour overlay** on the displayed phrase. Renders above each mora, OJAD-style, with the pattern chip.
9. **Up Next filmstrip.** Horizontally scrollable, current phrase highlighted, click jumps. Walk by ← / → arrow keys.
10. **Polish + responsive.** Mobile portrait collapse, waveform stacking, focus rings, keyboard navigation. Final pass against the four pedagogical pillars.

Each step is a working state. Stop at any step and the surface is still defensible.

## 7. Out of scope for v0.8 (named, not vague)

- **Server-side speech analysis.** No Whisper / Speechmatics / cloud STT until v1.0.
- **Saving user recordings across sessions.** Memory only.
- **Pronunciation scoring at the phoneme level** (e.g., "your /ɾ/ in りんご was too English"). Heuristic at the phrase level only.
- **Pitch correction suggestions.** ("Try dropping after の.") Visible in the contour comparison but no verbal coaching.
- **Multi-user / progress export / certificates.** Solo studio.
- **Custom phrase input.** Use the curated set; no user-submitted phrases.
- **Live conversation mode** (back-and-forth dialogue practice). Shadowing only.

These are deferred deliberately, not forgotten. v1.0 may revisit; v0.8 ships the immersive, teachable POC and proves the loop.
