# Speaking — Shadowing Studio · DESIGN.md

## Overview

Speaking is the first MOVE-and-LISTEN surface in Nihongo. Every other surface (Flavors, Edibles, Textures, Writing, Flashcards) trains receptive skills — see, hear, recognize. Speaking inverts the contract: the learner *produces*. That inversion shifts which design decisions matter. The page can't lean on the brand's natural patience without making the act of recording feel awkward; it can't lean on the brand's accent restraint without losing the obvious feedback the learner needs.

The brief resolves this by keeping the editorial chrome (paper, watercolor, brushed surfaces, three-role type, monochrome-with-restrained-accent) and dropping product mechanics INSIDE that chrome (mic, waveforms, score quartet, pitch overlay). The watercolor portrait gives the learner something to speak TO — a presence. The brushed-edge surfaces hold the practice stage. The four score chips report heuristically-real numbers without celebrating them.

The Pitch & Tones basics page is the pedagogical underwriter. The studio's contour line means nothing until the learner has been shown what UP and DOWN sound like; the basics page teaches that explicitly with worked examples in heiban/atamadaka/nakadaka/odaka, plus pause notation and basic intonation marks. Every contour overlay on the studio page links back to this basics surface.

## Colors

The speaking-specific palette adds five new color decisions on top of the project's foundation: a mic color world (umber default, vermilion recording, with a pulse halo), two waveform inks (umber for the model, sage for the user — the ONLY green in the entire app, lawful because the contrast is meaningful and the user's audio is the user's own input), a score chip ink (deep umber for the number, paper-deep for empty dots), and the four pitch-pattern accent colors that map to the four named patterns. The pitch LINE itself is plain ink — shape carries meaning; color is the pattern-type chip only.

Restraint everywhere else. No new background tints, no new card colors, no new section dividers. The page borrows existing tokens for paper, ink, and brushed edges.

## Typography

Three roles, all inherited from the project, used at speaking-specific sizes. The `display-phrase` size is the surface's signature: clamp(56px, 7vw, 96px) is large enough that the kana commands the eye but small enough that the OJAD contour above it can be drawn at sensible scale without crowding. The italic English gloss below sits at clamp(20px, 1.8vw, 28px) — distinctly secondary, never competes. The "PHRASE 2 / 6" eyebrow uses the project's small-caps EN label style at 12px with 0.18em tracking; it's a position indicator, not a header.

The mora-level glyphs (when a phrase is broken into its mora for the contour overlay) drop slightly to clamp(28px, 3.4vw, 42px) — smaller than display-phrase because the contour line and the pattern chip need clearance above each mora. The contour line draws above the baseline; the line never crosses a glyph.

## Elevation

No shadows. The page uses the existing project pattern of brushed-edge surfaces: a SVG brush-frame mask applied as a background-image to the practice stage, the portrait frame, and the Up Next rail. The mask softens the edges so the surface looks like a rectangle of slightly-thicker paper sitting on the canvas, not a box dropped on top.

The score chips don't carry edges or surfaces — they sit directly on the practice stage as inline tokens. The mic IS a circle, drawn with the umber color and the existing project's drop-shadow-as-a-filter pattern (not a box-shadow) so it can use mix-blend-mode against the watercolor without losing the depth cue. During recording, an OUTER ring expands and contracts on `dur-recording-pulse` as a sumi-e wash, again via filter, not box-shadow.

## Components

### 6.1. Watercolor portrait (left zone)

The portrait sits in the asymmetric hero slot. Its frame uses the brushed-edge mask. The portrait file is selected by the active category id (`images/speaking/portrait-${categoryId}.webp`). If the file 404s, the slot renders a paper-textured silhouette via the existing `image-slot` fallback. The portrait is non-interactive (per restriction in PRODUCT.md §4). On mobile (≤760px) it collapses to a small shoulder thumbnail at the top of the practice stage; the brushed edge is dropped to save chrome.

### 6.2. Practice stage (right zone)

A vertically-stacked composition inside a brushed-edge surface:

- **Eyebrow row.** Sakura-glyph + "PHRASE 2 / 6" indicator. Project's small-caps EN style. Sits 12px above the phrase.
- **Display phrase.** Kana at display-phrase size, optionally with kanji + furigana via `<ruby>`. The OJAD pitch contour draws above the mora row when the phrase has contour data (every food-category phrase will have it seeded). A small pattern chip sits to the right of the phrase (e.g., `[1]` for atamadaka, colored per the pattern token).
- **EN gloss.** Italic, below the phrase. Always present. Never bold.
- **Mic.** Centered, large (88×88 default, 96×96 on touch). Two states: default (umber, microphone glyph) and recording (vermilion, pulse ring). Tap to start; tap again to stop. Auto-stops at 8s of silence. The label below ("録音する · Tap to Record") changes to ("録音中… · Recording") when active.
- **Waveform pair.** Two horizontal tracks, stacked. Top: "お手本 · Original" (umber waveform, derived from a synthesized representation since Web Speech API doesn't expose its audio buffer — the line shape is computed deterministically from the kana mora count + pitch contour, looks like a real waveform but is generated). Bottom: "あなた · Your Version" (sage waveform, REAL data from the MediaRecorder's AudioContext analyser). Each row has a small play triangle on the left. Recording duration shows on the right (e.g., "00:02 / 02:10").
- **Score quartet.** Four chips in a row: Rhythm / Clarity / Pitch / Naturalness. Each chip shows a small glyph (motion-line / sparkle / pitch-curve / leaf), the JA label, the EN label italic, and a 5-dot strip filled per score (0–5 dots). The rolling session score sits in a circle at the right end ("92 / 100") — present but secondary.

### 6.3. Up Next filmstrip (bottom zone)

Horizontal scrollable rail. Each card carries: a phrase number, the kana, an italic EN gloss. Current phrase highlighted with a brush-circle around the number; past phrases get a small checkmark glyph; future phrases sit at default tone. Click jumps to that phrase. ← / → arrow keys walk. The rail breathes with the existing rail-of-cards pattern from the Edibles items grid — same gap rhythm, same hover lift.

### 6.4. Pitch contour overlay

For every phrase displayed in the studio AND on the Pitch & Tones basics page worked examples:

- The line draws above the mora row, starting at "low" (a baseline 12px above the glyph cap height) and "high" (a level 24px above the glyph cap height — 12px of vertical travel).
- For each mora, the line is either flat-low, flat-high, or includes a vertical drop/rise between this mora and the next.
- Convention: heiban = low → high → high → high (no fall); atamadaka = high → low → low (fall after mora 1); nakadaka = low → high → ... → low (fall mid-word); odaka = low → high → ... → high → low (fall after the last mora of the word, only audible with the particle).
- The line is plain ink color. The pattern chip beside the phrase carries the color cue per the pitch-pattern-* tokens.
- On the studio screen, after the user records, an additional sage-colored line overlays the user's pitch (extracted via autocorrelation on the user's audio buffer) — visible side-by-side with the model. This is the diagnostic the pitch-aware re-learner persona is here for.

### 6.5. Score dots

Each score chip carries a 0–5 dot strip beside its number. Filled dots use `score-dot-filled` (warm umber); empty dots use `score-dot-empty` (paper-deep). The number above the dots is the actual score (0–100). Dots are a visual rounding — a 92 reads as 5 filled, an 87 reads as 4 + 1, etc. The dots make the chip readable without parsing the number; the number is there for the pitch-aware re-learner who wants precision.

### 6.6. Pitch & Tones basics page

Single scrollable editorial surface under Writing. Sections:
- **Header**: 「ピッチアクセント・トーン」+ italic EN intro.
- **Why pitch matters**: 2 paragraphs explaining that Japanese is mora-timed and pitch-accented; the same kana can mean two different things depending on pitch (橋 hashi 'bridge' [2] vs 箸 hashi 'chopsticks' [1] vs 端 hashi 'edge' [0]).
- **The four patterns**: heiban / atamadaka / nakadaka / odaka. Each with the canonical example, the contour drawn at full size, the pattern chip, and a play button that runs Web Speech TTS on the example. Worked examples use the existing pitch-pattern-* color tokens for the chip.
- **Pauses & notation**: brief legend explaining the OJAD line, the small drop arrow, the comma vs space pause distinction, the rising-question intonation.
- **Practice link**: footer link → Speaking / Shadowing.

The basics page uses the existing Writing-section layout (rail-and-body inside an editorial single-scroll), inheriting from particles and sentence-structure pages. No new chrome.

## Do's and Don'ts

**Do** render the pitch contour on every phrase that carries contour data, on phrase 1 of session 1 and on phrase 200. Repetition trains the eye.

**Do** keep the mic the visual signature of the right column. Everything else can fall away in the layout hierarchy; the mic stays at the center.

**Do** use the sage waveform/pitch color ONLY in the speaking surface. It earns its existence here because it's the user's own input differentiator; it would clash everywhere else in the app.

**Do** auto-delete the user's audio buffer on phrase change. RAM hygiene, restriction §4.

**Don't** add emojis to score feedback. (Restriction.)

**Don't** invent a new pitch notation. OJAD-style only. (Restriction.)

**Don't** play recording-success sounds or chime effects. The waveform comparison IS the feedback.

**Don't** render the Up Next as a numbered list with bullets. The phrases are cards on a filmstrip, not a to-do list.

**Don't** make the watercolor portrait interactive. (Restriction.)

**Don't** let the score dots ever pulse, blink, or animate beyond the initial fill-in cascade after a recording lands.

**Don't** put the model waveform on top of the user waveform (overlay). They are stacked, not overlaid. The user needs to compare shape, not read a chord.
